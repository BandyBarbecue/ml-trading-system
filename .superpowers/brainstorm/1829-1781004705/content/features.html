Section 3: Feature Engineering

Six signals, computed daily for all 500 stocks. Everything is cross-sectionally normalised before entering the model.

Price-based signals (from Alpaca OHLCV)

Momentum 1m / 3m / 12m Return over 21 / 63 / 252 days, but skip the last 5 trading days. The skip prevents short-term reversal from contaminating momentum — a well-known artefact. Academic name: Jegadeesh-Titman momentum.
Mean Reversion 1w Negative of the 5-day return. Stocks that dropped sharply last week tend to bounce; those that rallied tend to cool off. Opposite sign to momentum — they capture different time horizons.
Realised Vol 20d Standard deviation of daily returns over 20 days. Used two ways: as a feature itself (low-vol stocks historically outperform) and as the denominator in HRP position sizing.

⭐ Claude NLP Pipeline — the cutting-edge part

Run once per day before market open. Processes overnight headlines in batches of 20 to stay within API rate limits.

Fetch headlines

Claude extraction

Aggregate to one score per ticker per day. Recent headlines weighted 2× older ones. Propagate to supply-chain tickers.

Cost estimate: ~500 headlines/day × ~200 tokens each = ~100k tokens/day ≈ $0.30/day at Haiku 4.5 pricing. Affordable for a research system.

Earnings Surprise (from FMP)

Standardised Unexpected Earnings (SUE): (actual_EPS − estimated_EPS) / std(last_8_surprises). Standardising prevents a high-SUE stock from dominating just because its EPS is volatile. The signal decays over 60 days — a big beat from last quarter still carries some weight today, just less.

Cross-sectional normalisation — why it matters

Every feature is z-scored within the S&P 500 on each day before entering the model. Analogy: instead of asking "did Apple go up 5%?", we ask "was Apple in the top 20% of all stocks today?" This removes market-wide moves (everything goes up in a bull market) and makes the model rank stocks against each other — which is what we actually want. It also makes the model robust across different market regimes.
