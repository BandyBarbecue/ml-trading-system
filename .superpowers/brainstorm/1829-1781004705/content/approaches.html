Three architectures for your system

All use S&P 500 + news sentiment + earnings surprise. The difference is how signals combine and when trades fire.

A

Multi-Factor ML Fusion ⭐ Recommended

Every stock gets a daily score from 4–6 features: price momentum (1m/3m/12m), short-term mean reversion, news sentiment (NLP on Alpaca headlines), and earnings surprise (FMP). A LightGBM model — trained on historical data — learns which combination of these predicts 20-day forward returns best. Rank all 500 stocks by predicted return, go long the top 20–30. Retrain monthly on a rolling 2-year window. Regime filter scales position sizes down in high-volatility markets.

Pros

  • Academically grounded — each factor has decades of evidence
  • ML learns signal weights from data, not guesswork
  • Diversified across 20–30 positions
  • Walk-forward prevents overfitting

Cons

  • Needs ~2 years of training data per retrain cycle
  • Feature engineering takes time to get right
B

News-First Event-Driven

The NLP pipeline is the gatekeeper. A significant news event (earnings beat, CEO change, FDA approval) must be detected first. If sentiment score crosses a threshold, a position opens. Price momentum and volatility are used as confirmation filters — don't buy into a news spike if the stock is already parabolic. Fewer trades but each is high-conviction. More like a systematic version of how discretionary traders operate.

Pros

  • Intuitive — humans already trade news this way
  • High signal-to-noise per trade
  • Interpretable: "I bought because X happened"

Cons

  • Sparser trades → less data to validate on
  • Event detection threshold is hard to calibrate
  • Misses slow-moving fundamental trends
C

Regime-Switching Multi-Strategy

A regime detector (Hidden Markov Model or VIX-based) classifies the market each day: bull/trending, range-bound, or crisis. A different sub-strategy activates per regime: momentum in bull markets, mean-reversion + news in sideways markets, defensive (SPY short or cash) in crises. Each regime has its own position-sizing rules. Most adaptive, but most complex to build and validate.

Pros

  • Adapts to market conditions — doesn't run momentum into a crash
  • Can survive 2008, 2020, 2022

Cons

  • Three sub-systems to build and validate
  • Regime detection itself can overfit
  • Much more complex to backtest correctly

⭐ Recommendation: A. It's the architecture used by systematic hedge funds like AQR and Two Sigma at their core. ML learns the right signal weights. The 2-month timeline is achievable. B and C are good extensions to add on top of A later.
