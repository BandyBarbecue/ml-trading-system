Cutting edge: what's real vs what's hype

The architecture is already close to what AQR and Two Sigma use at their core. One upgrade makes it genuinely state-of-the-art in 2026.

✓ Already cutting edge

Cross-sectional ML on factors Ranking stocks against each other (not predicting raw prices) is exactly the AQR / Renaissance approach. Much more robust than price prediction.
LightGBM on tabular features In 2026, gradient boosting still beats deep learning on tabular financial data. This is well-established. Don't let anyone sell you a transformer for this.
Walk-forward validation The correct way to validate any strategy. Most "AI trading" projects skip this and overfit. You won't.
Post-earnings drift signal One of the most persistent market anomalies. Still works. Used by every serious quant fund.

⭐ The upgrade that makes it state-of-the-art: swap FinBERT for Claude

You already have an Anthropic API key in your .env. FinBERT gives you one of three outputs: bullish / bearish / neutral. That's a blunt instrument. Here's what Claude gives you instead:

FinBERT output

"Apple beats Q3 earnings by 8%"

→ sentiment: POSITIVE

→ score: 0.91


That's it. No context.

Claude structured extraction

{

 "event_type": "earnings_beat",

 "primary_ticker": "AAPL",

 "affected_tickers": ["TSMC","QCOM"],

 "magnitude": "large",

 "time_horizon": "1-4 weeks",

 "confidence": 0.87,

 "reasoning": "Beat driven by services margin..."

}

This is what quant hedge funds with LLM access are doing right now. The affected_tickers field is the killer feature — Apple earnings affect TSMC and Qualcomm as suppliers. FinBERT can't see that. Claude can.

Two more upgrades worth adding

Dynamic factor weights Track each factor's Information Coefficient (IC) — how predictive has momentum been in the last 60 days? Reweight LightGBM inputs accordingly. The model adapts when regimes shift.
Hierarchical Risk Parity sizing More sophisticated than inverse-vol. Considers correlations between your 25 positions — doesn't let 10 tech stocks dominate just because they have similar vol. Ray Dalio's approach.
