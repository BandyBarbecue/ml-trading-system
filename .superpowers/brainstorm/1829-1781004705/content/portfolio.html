Section 5: Portfolio Construction

From 25 scored stocks to actual position sizes. HRP ensures you're genuinely diversified — not just spread across 25 stocks that all move together.

Why not just equal-weight the top 25?

If your top 25 includes AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, META, GOOGL, AMZN — that's 6 mega-cap tech stocks all moving together. Equal weight gives them 24% of the portfolio. When tech sells off, you take 6 simultaneous hits. HRP sees that these stocks are a correlated cluster and treats the whole cluster as one allocation unit, capping the damage.

Analogy: you wouldn't call a portfolio "diversified" just because it has 25 lines — not if 15 of those lines are different flavours of the same bet.

How HRP works — four steps

STEP 1

Correlation matrix

60-day rolling returns of your 25 stocks. Which pairs move together?

AAPL–MSFT: 0.82
AAPL–JPM: 0.31
AAPL–XOM: 0.18
STEP 2

Cluster similar stocks

Hierarchical clustering groups correlated stocks. Tech forms one cluster. Financials another. Healthcare another.

[AAPL MSFT NVDA META]
  ↕ tech cluster
[JPM GS BAC]
  ↕ finance cluster
[JNJ PFE MRK]
STEP 3

Recursive bisection

Split into two halves. Allocate inversely proportional to each half's variance. Recurse within each half.

Tech cluster (high vol)
→ gets less weight

Healthcare cluster (low vol)
→ gets more weight
STEP 4

Final weights

Weights sum to 1. Each cluster gets fair representation regardless of how many stocks are in it.

AAPL: 4.2%
MSFT: 3.9%
JPM: 6.1%
JNJ: 7.3%
∑ = 100%

Sector hard cap

Even after HRP, if any GICS sector exceeds 30% of portfolio value, we scale those positions down proportionally and redistribute the freed weight. This prevents concentrated sector bets even when the ML strongly favours one industry.

Example: HRP gives tech 38% → cap to 30% → 8% redistributed to other sectors pro-rata.

Weekly rebalance

Every Monday before open: re-run ML predictions → re-run HRP → compare to current holdings → generate trade list. Turnover cap: 40% of portfolio per rebalance.

If the optimal portfolio requires 60% turnover, we execute only the highest-priority trades first (biggest position changes). This keeps transaction costs realistic.

OUTPUT OF THIS LAYER

A dictionary of {ticker: target_weight} — e.g. {"AAPL": 0.042, "JPM": 0.061, ...}. Passed to the risk engine for final veto before orders are placed.
